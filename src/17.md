# Chapter 17: Immune to the Contagion

Someone snaps at you.

Whether it is a partner whose voice suddenly sharpens over something small, a colleague firing a cutting remark across a meeting table, or a stranger on the other end of the phone, the dynamic is the same. The words are sharp, the heat is real, and in the instant it lands, something fires in you too.

Your pulse kicks, your jaw tightens, the heat rises in your chest and neck—and right behind it, the Big Monster's voice arrives, the same voice you've been learning to see through all book, but now aimed at a different target.

*They just attacked you and can't talk to you like that, so you can't let this pass and must fire back right now.*

This contagion occurs when their heat becomes your heat, and their snap demands your snap in return. The whole thing happens in seconds, and if you weren't paying attention, you'd be in the middle of it before you knew you'd caught anything.

But you've been paying attention—you've spent this entire book learning to see the mechanism. And now you can see it running in someone else. That changes everything.



When you snapped at people across all those times, triggers, and sharp words, were you delivering a careful, considered truth about them? Were you offering an accurate, measured assessment of who they were and what they deserved, or were you discharging heat through a narrowed nervous system?

You know the answer—you weren't delivering truth. You were running a script: the Little Monster fired, the Big Monster supplied the story, and the words came out, not because they were accurate, but because they were available. The person in front of you wasn't the real target—they were just the nearest surface for the heat to hit.

So here's the question that changes everything: if that's what was happening when you snapped, what's happening when someone snaps at you?

The answer is the same—the person snapping at you is not a fully present, fully choosing human being delivering a considered judgment about you. They are someone whose Little Monster has fired and whose Big Monster is running its script.  The other person is hijacked, exactly the way you used to be, and their words are not an accurate read of you. They're the discharge of a narrowed nervous system, aimed at whatever happened to be in front of the heat.



Their snap was never truly about you; it was driven by their heat, their trigger, and their Big Monster's story. The sharp words landed on you, but they weren't aimed at you with any clarity. They were aimed at the nearest target; you just happened to be there.

## What Their Snap Is Actually For

Let's go a step further, because you now know exactly what their snap is doing.



Their trigger fired, their Big Monster interpreted it as an emergency—*I'm being disrespected, this is an attack, I must respond now*—and that interpretation created a wave of internal pressure. The snap was the release, the sharp words let the pressure out, they felt better for a few seconds. And then, just like it always did for you, the regret arrived later—or didn't, because their story kept justifying it.



You don't need to defend against it, not because it isn't unpleasant or the words don't sting, but because defending against it would be like defending against a wave. The wave isn't attacking you; it's just moving. The snap isn't a considered attack—it's a symptom.

## The Immunity

So here's what becomes possible when you see the other person's snap clearly.

The bait is still there, the sharp words still land, and your Little Monster may still fire—that's the body's ancient wiring, and it doesn't unlearn its patterns just because you understand what's happening. Your pulse may still kick and your heat may still rise.

But the Big Monster's pitch—*fire back now, they attacked you, you can't let this pass*—has no authority anymore. You are not suppressing it, clamping down, counting to ten, or biting your tongue; rather, the story that used to make the counter-strike feel necessary has already been dismantled.

You don't catch their heat because there's nothing left in you that believes their heat is an attack that demands your participation. Their Little Monster fires, their Big Monster runs its script, and you watch it—the way you'd watch a storm from behind a window. The wind is real, the noise is real, but it doesn't have to come inside.

 Just the quiet clarity that comes from seeing the mechanism, their mechanism, in real time, and recognizing it for what it is.

## The Ultimate Strength



Not taking the bait is not an act of passivity or weakness, nor does it mean accepting the attack, tolerating disrespect, or letting the other person walk all over you.



Think about what the other person is showing you when they snap—their loss of control. They're showing you that their nervous system has been hijacked, that their Big Monster is running the show, that a trigger was enough to knock them off their footing. That's not strength; that's vulnerability, wearing a costume.

Now think about what you're showing when you stay steady—you're showing something the other person can't touch. You're not rattled, you're not pulled into their storm; their heat doesn't become your heat, their script doesn't become your script. You're standing outside the whole mechanism, watching it spin, and refusing to climb in.



The person who snaps is showing you their buttons; the person who stays steady is showing the other person something they can't push.

You don't have to catch what they're throwing—and once you see that clearly, the last obstacle before the final step is gone.

Their snap was simply their nervous system firing, meaning you don't have to catch what they're throwing.

So you're immune to the contagion. But there's one more thing to see before the final step: nothing was ever actually at stake by waiting. That's the next chapter.
